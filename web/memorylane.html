How it works

It remembers the days like today.

Not just "on this day". MemoryLane weighs place, season, weather and time of day, so a rainy November evening by the lake brings back the rainy evenings you spent there before.

  1. 1

    It reads right now

    Your location (with your permission), the date, the time and today's weather from Open-Meteo. You can also time-travel to any other day.

  2. 2

    It finds your days like today

    Every photo gets a score: how close it was taken, how near the same week of the year, whether the sky was the same and whether it was the same hour. Photos from one outing become one memory.

  3. 3

    It plays them back

    Each memory says why it surfaced: "3 years ago today · 250 m from here · also a rainy day". Then sit back and watch.

Three ways to relive it

A film, a wall of photos, a book you can hold.

Film

A cinematic slideshow with slow pan-and-zoom, soft cross-fades and a title card for every year. Plays by itself, full screen.

Grid

Every match at a glance, grouped by year, with the reason each photo came back. Tap Play for a full-screen show.

Photo book

Magazine-style spreads with a cover, a chapter per year and real page turns. Turns its own pages, or print it to PDF.

Music that matches the sky

A soundtrack composed live in your browser follows today's weather: Golden hour, Soft overcast, Rain on glass, First snow or Night drive. Or play your own song.

Private by design

Your photos never leave your device.

  • Read on-device. Dates, places and thumbnails are worked out in your browser and kept in its private storage.
  • No account, no upload. There is no MemoryLane server holding your pictures.
  • Only what you choose. You pick the folder, the photos or the archive. Location is used only with your permission.
  • Gone when you say so. Clear the library with one tap.
FAQ

Questions

Is MemoryLane free?

Yes. It runs in any modern browser on your phone, tablet or computer, and you can add it to your home screen like an app.

Do you see my photos?

No. Photos are read and stored in your own browser. Nothing is sent to us. Only public photos from Wikimedia Commons are loaded from the internet, and only if you ask for them.

Why don't Instagram or Facebook connect directly?

Those networks no longer let apps read your personal photos. Request the copy of your data they offer ("Download your information"), then drop the ZIP into MemoryLane. It keeps each photo's date and, where the export has it, the place.

My Google Photos have no location. Why?

Google removes location from photos shared through its picker, so those match on date, time and weather. For places, import a Google Takeout download instead, which keeps them.

What if I'm somewhere new?

MemoryLane still surfaces photos from the same week in past years, and you can time-travel to any date. Or try "Around here, years ago" for public photos taken near you.
